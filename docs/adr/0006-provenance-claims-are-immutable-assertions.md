# Provenance is recorded as immutable claims with partial dates

An Object's provenance is a set of Provenance claims (`provenanceClaims`), not an editable ownership list. Each claim keeps its author's wording, holder, role (owner or custodian, or unstated), method, place, dates and confidence exactly as recorded. A correction records a new claim that supersedes the old one, and a retraction ends a claim without replacing it; both keep the original. Contradictory claims stay side by side, optionally marked as contradicting each other, and a contradiction follows corrections to the current claim. A reviewer with `collections.review_provenance` accepts, disputes or rejects claims; each decision is appended to `provenanceReviews` and never edits the claim. A claim that was reviewed or is contested can only be retracted by a reviewer, so it cannot leave review unnoticed. Evidence (`provenanceEvidence`) is appended to a claim as a citation or a document from the Object's media; its citation, excerpt and file-name snapshot are never rewritten, and a mistaken link is retracted with a reason. A claim recorded as documented must keep at least one evidence item.

Historical dates are stored as structured partial dates: a precision (day, month, year or decade), the known parts, an optional qualifier (circa, not after, not before) and an "uncertain" flag. They are never converted to timestamps, because a timestamp would invent a day the source does not give. They map directly to EDTF for interchange.

Holders reuse Party links (ADR 0002 retention applies). A named holder may be linked to a Party through a `partyLinks` row that targets the claim. These links carry no effective dates, because the claim holds them. Anonymous holders ("private collection") and unknown holders (a gap in the record) are stated on the claim and never become invented Parties. Identifying an anonymous holder later is a new claim that supersedes the anonymous one.

Provenance follows its Object. When an Object is transferred to another organization, its claims, evidence and review decisions go with it: re-homing changes which organization holds a claim, never what the claim says. Party links stay with the sending organization, whose accounts they name, and the claim keeps its holder as written. If the receiver corrects such a claim and the Object later returns, the sender's link still points at the version it was made on; the sender links the Party to the correction again if it still applies. A long history moves in batches, which a further transfer can overtake. Until the last batch arrives from every organization the Object left, the receiver is told that part of it is still on its way, its provenance and the Parties linked to it cannot be changed, and an image that evidence on its way cites cannot be deleted, so nothing is decided from part of the record. Provenance that transfers left behind before it moved with its Object counts as on its way until the repair of past transfers moves it.

The legacy free-text `provenance` field, `ownershipHistory` and the fine-art `provenanceChain` are kept unchanged. The free text and ownership history are shown beside the claims, and staff can turn them into claims; the fine-art chain stays on its own tab. Nothing is migrated automatically, because an automatic migration would turn unsourced text into apparently structured assertions.
