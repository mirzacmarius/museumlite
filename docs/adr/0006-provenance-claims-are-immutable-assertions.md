# Provenance is recorded as immutable claims with partial dates

An Object's provenance is a set of Provenance claims (`provenanceClaims`), not an editable ownership list. Each claim keeps its author's wording, holder, role (owner or custodian), method, place, dates and confidence exactly as recorded. A correction records a new claim that supersedes the old one, and a retraction ends a claim without replacing it; both keep the original. Contradictory claims stay side by side, optionally marked as contradicting each other, until a reviewer with `collections.review_provenance` accepts, disputes or rejects them. Review never edits the claim. Evidence (`provenanceEvidence`) is appended to a claim as a citation or a document from the Object's media; its citation, excerpt and file-name snapshot are never rewritten, and a mistaken link is retracted with a reason. A claim recorded as documented must keep at least one evidence item.

Historical dates are stored as structured partial dates: a precision (day, month, year or decade), the known parts, an optional qualifier (circa, not after, not before) and an "uncertain" flag. They are never converted to timestamps, because a timestamp would invent a day the source does not give. They map directly to EDTF for interchange.

Holders reuse Party links (ADR 0002 retention applies). A named holder may be linked to a Party through a `partyLinks` row that targets the claim. These links carry no effective dates, because the claim holds them. Anonymous holders ("private collection") and unknown holders (a gap in the record) are stated on the claim and never become invented Parties. Identifying an anonymous holder later is a new claim that supersedes the anonymous one.

The legacy free-text `provenance` field, `ownershipHistory` and the fine-art `provenanceChain` are kept and shown as unstructured legacy text. Staff can turn that text into claims. Nothing is migrated automatically, because an automatic migration would turn unsourced text into apparently structured assertions.
