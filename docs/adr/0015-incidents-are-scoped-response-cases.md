# Incidents are scoped response cases guided only by plans staff choose

An incident is a case: a report of damage, loss, theft or an emergency that affects collection objects or locations, from rapid intake through triage and response to an after-action record. Any member who can read the collection can report one, because the person who finds a leak is rarely the one who coordinates the response. Coordinating (triage, choosing the lead and responders, choosing plans, recording the after-action review and reopening) needs the new `collections.coordinate_incidents` permission together with `collections.update`. Responders work through case tasks (MUS-656) on the incident like on any other case, and their notifications go through the one dispatcher (ADR 0007).

A sensitive incident is visible only to coordinators, its reporter, its lead and the responders named on it. Everyone else gets "not found" from every incident query and mutation, its tasks drop out of their My work and review queues, and they cannot be made task owners or reviewers on it. The organization audit log keeps who did what and when, but for a sensitive incident it records the incident number instead of task titles, and movement reasons name only the incident number. The incident's own event log, which only people in scope can read, holds the detail.

Response plans are institution configuration. They are preparation recipes (ADR 0012) with the `incident_response` trigger, written by a member with `organization.manage_settings`; only active ones can be chosen. The product ships no starter plan for incidents. A coordinator chooses at triage which plans guide the response, or explicitly records that none does; nothing chooses a plan automatically, not even when only one exists. The chosen plans' steps become suggested tasks keyed on the incident, recipe and step, so triaging again or adding a plan later never duplicates a step. Step due dates count hours from triage instead of days, because emergency work is measured in hours.

Repeated triggers resolve to one incident. Intake carries a key the client generates once per report form, so a retried or double-submitted report returns the first incident. Reporting from an environmental alert episode (ADR 0013) records the episode as the incident's source, and reporting from the same episode again opens the existing incident. Notifications are keyed on the incident and the event's position in its log.

Evacuation and recovery are physical handoffs recorded by the guarded movement command (MUS-628) with their own movement kinds, the incident on the movement row, the observed source and the mover's `locations.update` permission. A handoff carries a client key, so a retry after a lost response returns the first movement instead of failing on the changed source. Nothing moves an object because an incident was triaged, a task was completed or the incident was closed; the suggested recovery destination is the location recorded when the object was linked to the incident, and staff confirm it.

Linked evidence (accepted condition assessments and object files of affected objects, insurance claims) is retained: a mistaken link is retracted with a reason and stays visible as retracted. Objects are never unlinked from an incident; staff record them as unaffected. Each viewer sees an evidence label only with the read access that record needs, and insurance coverage of affected objects only with insurance access. An incident closes only with an after-action record and no open tasks; reopening keeps the earlier record in the log.

Considered and rejected: a separate response plan table, because it would duplicate the recipe editor, owner rules and once-per-step suggestion keys; applying every active plan when an incident is triaged, because it lets automation decide the response; per-incident access lists for every incident, because most incidents should be visible to all collection staff; and redacting sensitive incidents from the audit log entirely, because the institution still needs to know who acted.
