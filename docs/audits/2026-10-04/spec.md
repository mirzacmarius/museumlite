## Problem Statement

Museum staff need one trustworthy record of an object's identity, custody, documentation and institutional history throughout acquisition, accession, cataloguing, assessment, storage, movement, exhibition, loan, conservation, valuation, insurance and disposal. MuseumLite already provides many of these modules, but alternative execution paths apply inconsistent permissions, relationship validation, history and lifecycle rules. Staff must reconcile conflicting states and repeat information between modules.

The audit demonstrated cross-organization disclosure, anonymous environmental writes, public disclosure of internal documents, cross-organization location assignment without movement evidence, retry-created accession duplicates, misleading report success and incorrect search results. Permanent deletion paths also erase the evidence explaining institutional decisions. These problems undermine confidentiality, collection care and trust in the record. Incomplete queues, partial history, unbounded scans and inconsistent metrics become more consequential as collections grow.

This specification turns the audit into an ordered museum-first improvement program. It does not authorize application changes. The user requires explicit approval of each implementation batch and complete implementation, verification and reporting after approval.

## Solution

Retain the existing application platform and strengthen its shared authorization, evidence and workflow boundaries before expanding features. Give staff complete, connected procedures around one stable object identity; preserve permanent records and evidence; require explicit file-level public approval; and receive environmental readings through authenticated, retry-safe ingestion.

Implement the program in eight approval-gated batches, beginning with security, permanent-record protection and a dependable backend compiler gate. Follow with intake integrity, custody and condition, trustworthy history/reporting/scale, parties and financial/legal context, daily UX, automation and optional professional extensions. Each delivered feature includes functioning backend, schema/migrations where needed, validation, permissions, UI, recovery behavior and meaningful workflow verification.

Museums drive the product. Library circulation remains optional. Spectrum 5.1 is a procedure-coverage reference, without a compliance claim or an assumed jurisdiction. Support small teams with combined roles and larger institutions with separate responsibilities and sites.

## Implementation Decisions

### Accepted constraints and architectural direction

- Specification publication and the ready-for-agent label are not implementation authorization. No batch is approved yet. An implementing agent must obtain the user's explicit batch approval, implement only that batch completely, and stop for review before another batch. Do not infer approval from this issue's status.
- Retain the monorepo, Convex ownership of data/auth/storage/scheduled work, and Next.js UI/routing. A platform or full event-sourced rewrite is not proposed.
- Preserve existing institutional records, accession numbers, evidence and historical assertions. Use additive schema/index changes, bounded rerunnable migrations, legacy states and reviewed reconciliation. Do not auto-merge parties, auto-renumber historical duplicates, infer missing custody or delete original evidence.
- Withdraw/archive museum records with restore. Guard indirect deletion and organization cleanup, as well as visible trash actions. Withdrawal, physical disposal and deaccession remain distinct.
- Publish files individually through approved version-specific public renditions. Documents and unknown/restricted rights default private. Original-download access is a separate decision; missing derivatives never fall back to originals.
- Validate the actual tenant and access for every related ID before reading or writing. Typed identifiers and authorization of the first batch member are insufficient. Revalidate delayed jobs at execution.
- Prefer domain commands that combine permission, reference validation, state transition, evidence and projection updates transactionally. Retain append-only operational events alongside existing data, with original historical evidence preserved.
- Keep object identity stable through all cases. Collection groups are curatorial memberships; organizations are tenants; sites are subdivisions; parties are person/institution identities with contextual roles. Acquisition/title, accession, custody entry/exit, deaccession and archival withdrawal are separate concepts. These broader glossary contracts remain proposed directions pending their batch decisions.
- Current monetization guidance favors catalog modules remaining free with paid workflow gates. This program does not change pricing or customer entitlements. Capability cleanup must preserve intentional existing access and make any later commercial policy change explicit.
- The user plans Tuya temperature/humidity devices after describing webhook-based readings. Exact model, gateway, current live status, payload and supported transport remain unverified. Preserve any live sender through verified authenticated cutover. Provide a complete generic receiver without claiming vendor connectivity. A Tuya-specific connector is complete only with a verified adapter and real supported device flow.
- The schema already accommodates light and UV measurements; they can share device ingestion when units and real devices are configured. Initial required measurements are temperature and relative humidity. Do not infer reporting intervals, scaling or custom-header support from the Tuya brand.
- Security rollback cannot reinstate unsafe publication, anonymous device writes or permanent-record purging. Removing a future public projection does not recall a previously distributed original URL; rollout must assess historical exposure and retention-safe storage protection.

### Prioritized implementation contracts

The following entries retain the audit's recommendation identities. They are proposed implementation contracts, not claims that every later product policy has been accepted. Complexity is Small, Medium or Large. Risk describes the chance of breaking current behavior during change; priority describes the consequence of leaving the problem unresolved.

#### P0 — critical problems

- **R01 — Tenant reads.** Media/loan mixed-ID batches and import diagnostics were reproduced as foreign-organization disclosures; relationship-by-entity reads also authorize only their first row in source review. Authorize every actual record and reject inconsistent batches. Modules: media, loans, imports, relationships and shared authorization. Data: no new table expected. UX: consistent forbidden/unavailable state and same-organization callers. Complexity: Small. Dependencies: none. Risk: Low.
- **R02 — Public file projection.** Anonymous public object responses expose internal documents/originals. Centralize approved file/version/rendition, rights and expiry checks across public lists, details, thumbnails and downloads. Modules: catalog/public portal, media versions/derivatives, public UI. Data: approval/version/rendition metadata and additive private-default migration. UX: preview, approve/unpublish and eligibility reasons. Complexity: Medium. Dependencies: R01 and accepted per-file policy. Risk: High for legacy galleries.
- **R03 — Device ingestion.** Caller-supplied sensor source bypasses login and writes readings/alerts. Authenticate HTTPS device ingestion; bind device/probe to tenant/location; validate type, units, timestamp and stable reading identity; deduplicate, limit requests and rotate/revoke credentials. Manual readings stay staff-authorized. Modules: environmental monitoring, HTTP, device management. Data: device bindings, hashed credentials and deduplication records/indexes. UX: one-time credential, device health, errors and rotation/revocation. Complexity: Medium for generic receiver; vendor adapter may extend scope. Dependencies: selected sender/model/gateway contract before live cutover. Risk: High for existing senders.
- **R04 — Reference integrity.** Bulk and exhibition location writes can trust foreign references and bypass history. Validate every related entity/blob against actual ownership and permitted state at write/execution. Every executed internal move records observed source and actor. Modules: catalog/bulk, locations/exhibitions, media/uploads, relationships and authorization. Data: upload ownership binding if needed, invalid-reference review results. UX: valid selectors, actionable conflicts and partial-batch outcomes. Complexity: Medium. Dependencies: R01; R10 later expands custody. Risk: Medium.
- **R05 — Permanent evidence.** Object purge cascades remove disposal, movement, condition, valuation and documentary evidence. Implement archival withdrawal/restore and guard every direct/indirect permanent-evidence deletion path. Modules: object/register/treatment lifecycle, cleanup and organization deletion. Data: additive withdrawal metadata and retained evidence relationships/snapshots. UX: reason, archive/register visibility, restore access and no museum empty-trash purge. Complexity: Medium. Dependencies: accepted permanent-record policy. Risk: High for intentional deletion behavior changes.

## Testing Decisions

- **User-confirmed boundaries:** existing convex-test public query/mutation/action APIs, the HTTP ingress boundary and representative real-browser staff/public workflows. Prefer these existing high-level seams over helper-specific test interfaces; no new production testing seam is required by default.
- A good test asserts externally meaningful results: permitted actor can complete the procedure, forbidden actor receives no data/write, retry creates one logical result, errors preserve entered/history data, and related case/object projections agree. Avoid tests mirroring internal call order, private helper structure or arbitrary component snapshots.
- Reuse the existing Convex schema/module harness, organization/user/permission fixtures, authenticated identities, in-memory storage and scheduled-function completion support. Fixture setup can establish initial rows; exercise real public operations for the behavior under test. Use internal boundaries only for a worker's externally observable output or unavoidable fixture/job execution, not to bypass authorization in the scenario.
- Prior art exists in accession, deaccession, loan, media, relationship-authorization, reporting and authentication-upgrade suites. Existing HTTP billing webhook tests demonstrate ingress testing; extend that style for environmental credentials, payloads, acknowledgement and refusal behavior. Existing app tests and the shared collaborative browser cover presentation/routing journeys.
- Convert the nine audit reproduction probes into negative regression scenarios asserting safe behavior: foreign import diagnostics; replayed accession/progress; unsupported report success; foreign bulk location/history; mixed media lookup; unrelated identifier search; anonymous sensor write; internal public attachment; mixed loan metadata. The existing probes intentionally asserted defects; passing those same assertions after implementation would not prove a fix.
- Test tenant/role permutations at the API seam: authorized member, wrong role, nonmember, anonymous, and foreign IDs placed first/middle/last or mixed with missing IDs. Include referenced parties, locations, blobs and case links; UI hiding is insufficient.
- Test sensor HTTP behavior with synthetic devices/gateways and credentials, supported units, valid/invalid types/timestamps, repeated stable identities, distinct reading identities, late/out-of-order data, rate limits, rotation/revocation and location binding. No credentials in logs/fixtures committed to the project; no real cloud changes required for these tests.
- Test public-file behavior anonymously through every public response boundary and browser consumer: legacy/private/restricted/expired/unapproved/version-changed assets, valid approved rendition, missing rendition, unpublish and separately controlled originals. Do not assume changing a projection invalidates an already distributed original URL.
- Test withdrawal/restore with linked accession, disposal, loan, condition, treatment, movement, valuation and files. Assert retention/identity and authorized conflict recovery, plus indirect cleanup protection. Migration rehearsal checks counts and evidence rather than inventing missing history.
- Test the cross-module lifecycle from object intake and policy approval through catalog, condition, confirmed storage, loan/exhibition dispatch, transport/installation, receipt/return and post-treatment storage. Include partial returns, active occupancy, stale source, damage, unknown legacy origin and incoming-lender exit. Later valuation/coverage/disposal stages must reuse the same identity and retained evidence.
- Test queue/history/search completeness with more than one hundred intake records, more than one thousand audit entries and more than five thousand selectable objects, using bounded fixture generation. Test larger synthetic institutions to measure continuations, query/read budgets and complete artifact output; these counts are regression boundaries, not a capacity promise.
- Test report timezone/calendar behavior with controlled clocks, including DST and month boundaries; verify actual output and distinct delivery state, matched schedule identity, supported/unsupported generators and metric drill-down consistency. Test multi-currency/effective-date coverage with explicit expected totals.
- Test event/task/notification/delivery retries with fixture endpoints and controlled schedules: one logical intent, preference suppression, actual digests, sustained alert episode, transient/permanent failure and authorized replay. Do not send test messages to institutional recipients.
- Browser acceptance walks catalog/list context, permission variants, media/public preview, archive/restore, device status, operational forms and changed custody/reporting journeys. Include loading, empty/no-match, conflict/forbidden/unavailable, validation, interruption, keyboard and target mobile widths. Source-only responsive review cannot certify the UI.
- Required verification includes fresh lint, all workspace types, standalone backend compilation and complete relevant existing/new suites. Run production build, measured load and isolated migration/restore checks when the batch/release requires them. Inspect the actual diff and report failed or unavailable verification explicitly.

## Out of Scope

- Implementation, deployment, live migrations, sender credential provisioning or external message delivery triggered merely by publishing this specification. The user has not approved Batch 1 or any later batch.
- A rewrite of the platform, moving domain data/auth/storage/scheduled work out of Convex, or renaming all physical tables for terminology alone.
- Destructive record/blob backfills, automatic historical renumbering, unreviewed identity merges, invented locations/condition/title evidence or deletion of old synthetic disposal assertions.
- Assuming jurisdiction-specific legal compliance, accreditation, retention periods or Spectrum certification. Institution-specific policies are selected before their dependent workflows.
- Changes to pricing/customer entitlements or a new paid integration model without an explicit product decision.
- Assuming Tuya devices send configurable Bearer webhooks, choosing hardware/cloud subscriptions for the user, or claiming a built-in Tuya connector before the chosen upstream transport/adapter is verified. Lux/UV hardware rollout is not part of required temperature/humidity setup.
- A permissive anonymous compatibility fallback, original-file fallback when public derivatives are missing, fake controls, stub reports marked successful or unfinished features advertised as available.
- Automatically marking physical receipt, dispatch, return, installation, legal approval, ownership or condition improvement from a task/status/date change alone.
- Blanket release of all eight batches at once. Optional offline, emergency and research/reproduction work is roadmap scope requiring separate later approval, not Batch 1 delivery.
